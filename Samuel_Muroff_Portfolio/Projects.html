Here are two projects from my Information Science classes. One focuses on working with databases using SQL, and the other uses Power BI to turn sales data into easy-to-read visuals.

INFO 3401: SQL

These SQL queries use joins, filters, grouping, and subqueries to answer questions about sales and customers. They go beyond basic SELECT statements while staying easy to follow.

Which categories sell the most in U.S. stores?

SELECT TRIM(p.CategoryName) AS Category,
       SUM(s.Quantity) AS UnitsSold,
       COUNT(DISTINCT s.OrderKey) AS Orders
FROM factSales AS s
JOIN dimProduct AS p
  ON s.ProductKey = p.ProductKey
JOIN dimStore AS st
  ON s.StoreKey = st.StoreKey
WHERE st.CountryCode = 'US'
GROUP BY TRIM(p.CategoryName)
HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT s.OrderKey) >= 20
ORDER BY UnitsSold DESC
LIMIT 5

I joined three tables and filtered for U.S. stores. Then I counted units and unique orders by category, kept categories with at least 20 orders, and found the top five.

Which frequent customers buy the widest variety?

WITH CustomerOrders AS (
  SELECT CustomerKey,
         COUNT(DISTINCT OrderKey) AS Orders,
         COUNT(DISTINCT ProductKey) AS Products
  FROM factSales
  GROUP BY CustomerKey
)
SELECT c.GivenName, c.Surname,
       co.Orders, co.Products
FROM CustomerOrders AS co
JOIN dimCustomer AS c
  ON co.CustomerKey = c.CustomerKey
WHERE co.Orders > (
  SELECT AVG(Orders) FROM CustomerOrders
)
ORDER BY co.Products DESC, co.Orders DESC
LIMIT 10

I used a CTE to count each customer's orders and different products. A subquery finds the average number of orders, so I can rank customers who order more often by how many products they have tried.

INFO 3402: Power BI

For this project, I am creating ten report pages with the same set of sales data. I use the reports to explore products, stores, customers, and changes in sales over time.

01. How the four tables connect

The sales table holds each transaction. The product, customer, and store tables add details about what was sold, who bought it, and where it was sold.

The tables: factSales (transactions), dimProduct (product information), dimCustomer (customer information), and dimStore (store information).

02. Top-selling products by volume

This chart shows which products sold the most units. Comparing products within their category makes the results easier to understand.

03. Top-selling products by revenue

This chart focuses on total sales revenue instead of the number of products sold.

04. Top-selling stores by revenue

This view compares sales across stores, with online sales considered separately so physical stores are easier to compare.

05. Most profitable product categories

This chart compares the profit made by different product categories after product costs.

06. Repeat customers and their purchases

08. Sales and profit from 2021–2024
